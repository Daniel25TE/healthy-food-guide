Healthy Food Guide

Evidence-based nutrition information and affordable recipes for four chronic diseases. Content is based on guidance from the American College of Cardiology (2026 Scientific Statement), the National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute's DASH Eating Plan, the Cleveland Clinic, and the Mayo Clinic's DASH Diet Sample Menus.

Diabetes

Type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease are closely linked — about one-third of people presenting with heart disease also have diabetes. The Mediterranean diet is associated with an 8% lower risk of developing type 2 diabetes. Dietary carbohydrate quality and fiber intake are key factors in blood sugar control.

Heart Disease

Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death worldwide, accounting for approximately 20 million deaths annually. Diet and lifestyle change is considered a cornerstone of prevention and management. The Mediterranean and DASH dietary patterns have the strongest clinical trial evidence for reducing major cardiovascular events.

Obesity

Obesity is a major driver of type 2 diabetes, heart disease, and hypertension. Plant-forward dietary patterns — the Mediterranean and DASH diets — are supported by strong evidence for sustainable weight management. High-fiber foods promote satiety and help control calorie intake. Only 6% of Americans currently meet the recommended daily fiber intake.

Hypertension

High blood pressure is a leading risk factor for heart disease and stroke. The DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) diet was developed specifically to lower blood pressure through diet. Low-sodium eating reduces systolic blood pressure by an average of 6–8 mmHg — a clinically meaningful change achievable without medication.
